# Novel Curator — Initial Project Brief

## Problem
Can we help web-novel readers identify novels they would enjoy faster and more effectively than their existing discovery workflows?

Initial reference methods: Novel Updates filtering/rankings, r/noveltranslations recommendations, and suggestions from friends. These methods are not assumed to be ineffective; that needs investigation.

## Proposed intervention (hypothesis)
A responsive swipe-inspired card interface presenting novel title, synopsis, genres/tags, status, source link, and cover where permitted. Readers can **Skip** or **Shortlist**, then review their list.

The first test concerns the browsing interaction. The novel-selection approach will initially be simple; recommendation algorithms may be studied in later iterations.

## First release scope
Small sourced catalogue; card display; Skip; Shortlist; a deployed link; and a basic feedback route. No accounts, large-scale ingestion, LLM integration, or personalized model required initially.

## Evidence to gather
Interview readers about their *last real discovery session*; examine existing tools; identify bottlenecks; define a fair baseline; observe users with a working prototype. Candidate metrics: time to promising novel, cards examined, perceived relevance, and follow-up satisfaction. Define success criteria before claiming success.

## Team organization
The lead makes final decisions and delegates deliverables. Assigned owners propose and implement technical approaches, record trade-offs, coordinate interfaces and communicate blockers.

## Current uncertainties
- Does swipe-based browsing actually improve discovery?
- Which information is needed for a fair decision?
- Can lawful, accurate metadata be obtained affordably?
- Does a small catalogue constrain meaningful comparisons?
- How should actual reader interest and satisfaction be measured?

This brief is deliberately provisional and is not evidence of completed implementation.
